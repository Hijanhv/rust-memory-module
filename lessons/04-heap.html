The heap: Box and Vec

The heap is a big flexible area for data that's too large, changes size, or needs to outlive the function that made it.

In one sentenceThe heap holds the data, the stack holds a small pointer to it, and Rust frees the heap data when its owner goes away.

Stack vs heap, in one picture

The stack is your desk: small, tidy, everything in order. The heap is a storage warehouse: lots of room, but you have to ask for a spot, and you keep a ticket (the pointer) on your desk that says where your stuff is.

  1. You ask for heap memory (e.g. Box::new(5)).
  2. The allocator finds a free spot big enough, marks it as used, and gives back its address.
  3. That address (the pointer) is stored on the stack, in your variable.
  4. When your variable goes out of scope, Rust automatically tells the allocator "this spot is free again". This is called dropping.

Watch it: Box and vec!

Length vs capacity

A Vec on the stack is always 3 numbers (24 bytes):

  • ptr: where the items are on the heap
  • len: how many items are in it right now
  • capacity: how many items fit before it needs a bigger spot

Capacity is like a bookshelf with 4 slots. Length is how many books are on it. Add a 5th book and you need a new, bigger shelf: buy an 8-slot shelf, move all the books, throw the old shelf away.

Real output from my Mac

From cargo run --example 03_heap_box_vec. Look at when the pointer changes:

empty Vec: len=0 cap=0 ptr=0x4   (no heap allocation yet)
push  0: len= 1 cap= 4 ptr=0x104b613c0  <- new heap buffer
push  3: len= 4 cap= 4 ptr=0x104b613c0
push  4: len= 5 cap= 8 ptr=0x104b612d0  <- new heap buffer, data copied
push  8: len= 9 cap=16 ptr=0x104b612f0  <- new heap buffer, data copied
push 16: len=17 cap=32 ptr=0x104b61eb0  <- new heap buffer, data copied

with_capacity(20): pointer unchanged after 20 pushes? true

Three things to notice:

  1. An empty Vec doesn't touch the heap at all (0x4 is a placeholder, not real memory).
  2. Capacity doubles each time: 4, 8, 16, 32. That keeps copying rare.
  3. If you know the size up front, Vec::with_capacity(n) allocates once and never moves.

Who frees heap memory?

Popping a stack frame does not magically free heap memory by itself. Instead, the compiler inserts a drop call at the end of every scope for each owner. Drop for a Box or Vec tells the allocator to free its heap block. No garbage collector, no manual free().

Heap trade-offs

Good atBad at
Data of any size, even unknown until runtimeSlower: the allocator must search for space and keep records
Can grow and shrink (Vec, String)Fragmentation: lots of small holes can make big allocations hard
Can outlive the function that created itOwnership gets more complex, especially across threads
Can be shared (Rc, Arc)Scattered data is less cache friendly

Check yourself

let b = Box::new(5); What is on the stack and what is on the heap?Stack: the pointer b (8 bytes). Heap: the value 5 (4 bytes).
What are the three parts of a Vec on the stack?Pointer, length, capacity.
A Vec has len 4 and capacity 4. You push one more. What happens?It allocates a bigger heap block (capacity 8), copies the 4 items over, adds the new one, and frees the old block. The pointer changes.
If you kept a raw pointer to the old block, what would it point to after the push?Freed memory. Using it would be a use-after-free, the first bug in Lecture 2. Safe Rust won't let you keep a reference into a Vec while pushing to it.
